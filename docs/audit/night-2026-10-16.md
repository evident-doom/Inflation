# The night of 15–16 October 2026: rounds, trade, stable herds

All checks are hand-written moves on the wide map (2000 km), seed 1, unless said otherwise. "Runner" means checks run on the server by the runner session.

## Rounds (`-Dhex.rounds`, off by default)

**What works.** Herds keep to their rounds. By midsummer 50–70% of herds on a round are within 50 km of its place that month (`hex-rounds-check`). Rounds pass from parent to offshoot, drift a little, and are given up or taken up now and then, so herds with and without rounds compete.

**What does not work yet: rounds do not pay in most kinds.** The share of herds on a round falls from 50% to 15–35% over 8 years. It holds only in reindeer and hartebeest, and in the 30-year run reindeer on rounds reached 9.2k against 0.8k without.

Measured fairly (8 years, current head, cd35f38):

- Herds on a round eat as well and bear as many young as the others (fed 0.91–0.97 both ways; protein in the birth season 0.95–1.0 both ways).
- They are hunted 40–100% more, mostly by people, then by lions.
- Hunters stand 3–7 times thicker around them (for example oryx 63 against 12 kg/km², dromedary 116 against 24). This holds without people too (16 against 3.5).
- They stand with 30–60% more of their own kind in the hex, and go hungry about twice as often.

**What I tried, each as a switch.** None makes rounds pay on its own.

| Switch | Effect |
|---|---|
| `-Dhex.t.roundTrekDays=0` | Rounds spread and pay, but herds no longer keep to them (10–20% within 50 km) |
| `-Dhex.t.roundTrekDays=2` | In between |
| `-Dhex.t.roundArrived=100` | No better |
| `-Dhex.t.huntersHalf=30` | Herds tell thick hunter country from thin; no better |
| `-Dhex.t.roundSpread=60` | Each herd keeps its own part of a round's range; no better |
| `-Dhex.t.roundCue=0.05`, `0.15` | Herds set off only when the round's place is better; no better |
| `-Dhex.t.roundFollowGreen` | Rounds make for this year's best grazing in each range; no better |
| `-Dhex.t.denning` | Packs with cubs hunt within 15 km of their den for 3 months; the share on rounds holds better (gazelle 34%, onager 38%, oryx 38%, hartebeest 47%, dromedary 37%, against 27, 16, 24, 35, 26), but hunters still crowd round herds |

**Why.** A round brings a kind's herds to the same places at the same time each year. Hunters are drawn to where herds are each week (`herdPrey` in the hunters' sense of good country), and new packs settle on empty ground near game. So predictable gatherings draw predators, and above all people, who hunt there. That is true to life: people ambushed reindeer and gazelle on their routes. But real migrants also gain things the model lacks:

- Resident predators cannot follow them out of their territories, above all while they have young in the den.
- They swamp predators at calving.
- They leave the lean season's ground to predators that are short of food there.

**Decisions for Olli.**

1. **Packs tied to their dens** (tried behind `-Dhex.t.denning`). Do you want this as a mechanism? It is the classic reason migration escapes predation.
2. **Residents boom.** Roe deer grow ×11 in 10 years, wild boar ×8 and mouflon ×4, all well fed. The boom does not eat the land down (see below), but it feeds the predators all year, wherever they sit. Regulating residents (predators, winter, disease at density) would weaken that subsidy and should let rounds pay.

## Trade (`-Dhex.trade`, off by default; source rites need `-Dhex.rounds`)

**Materials.** Good knapping stone, salt and ochre are found only in some country. Salt lies by salt lakes and on desert shores.

| Material | What it does |
|---|---|
| Stone | Tools of poor stone serve at 0.75 and wear twice as fast |
| Salt | Cures meat and fish in any weather and keeps them; people living mostly on plants need it, and without it their food does them up to 15% less good |
| Ochre | Hides tanned with it wear half as fast |

**Gifts.** Bands give what they have beyond a year's need to bands that lack it. The gift binds both bands by how much of the other's want it meets, and gives the giver renown, which other bands count when they choose whose ways to copy.

**Rites.** A band's rites may take it to a source it knows, of something it lacks.

**Result.** `hex-trade-check`, 600 km, 5 years, seeds 91–93: people's score with gifts and source rites is 1.0573, 1.0648 and 1.0597, against 1.0475, 1.0621 and 1.0516 without them. In year 5, 18–36% of bands' dried food is salted, 66–87% of their spears are of good stone, and a fifth of rites stand at a source.

## Herd stability (defaults changed, tests pass)

1. **Protein floors** (5d01156). Grazers pick the best of dry grass (0.375 of fresh growth's protein, was 0.25), straw 0.2 (was 0.15), lichen 0.3 (was 0.2), and young short of protein die at half the rate. Runner, 10 years without people: onager, wild horse and reindeer hold, as with protein off.
2. **Trampling at a tenth** (cd35f38). This was the main cause.
   - It used to beat down about 40% of the shrubs a year at a tonne of animals per km². That is more than shrubs regrow, so the shrub layer halved in four years and the browsers starved.
   - The animals' own eating was only about 0.002 kg/m² a year (`hex-forage-check`).
   - Now, without people, edible forage stays (0.78 to 0.73 kg/m²) and every herd holds or grows on seed 1 and on seed 2 (runner).
   - With people, the herds now hold or fall far less. Hartebeest, dromedary and reindeer still fall 35–50% in 10 years from people's hunting.
3. **Still open.** Residents boom (above). Cheetah falls from about 1.5k to 0.1k, with or without these changes. Gazelle and ostrich grow 3–4× without people.
