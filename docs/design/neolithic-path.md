# The path to settling and farming: build plan

Agreed with Olli on 2026-10-14. This is the standing plan for people after the band-choices work. Keep the status column current, and work the steps in order unless Olli reorders them.

**Rules for every step:**
- Everything people do is a choice resolved by generic mechanisms ([band-choices.md](band-choices.md)). Nothing is scripted.
- Crops and animals are described by their properties, not by name, so that other paths can appear on other maps: tuber gardens, herding first, Jomon-style rich foraging.
- Each step ends with a check against the record, plus a test that the step's new choice is actually taken by the scaffold or learned ways where it pays, and not where it doesn't.

| # | Step | What it adds | Check | Status |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Ancestral places, calendars, gatherings** | Burials make a remembered place that draws its band back and stakes a claim. Recurring appointments: a moon count or season plus a place. Bands holding the same appointment converge. | Aggregation sites form at conspicuous places. Bands return to burial places. | done (2026-10-14): 2 in 3 bands hold appointments, about a third of due band-weeks kept, gatherings of 13–20 bands |
| 2 | **Base camps and houses** | Building a house is a choice paid in labour, making staying warmer, safer and better for storing. It brings storage pits, house mice eating stores, and middens. Staying already raises births and fouling. | Bands near rich, steady food (oaks, rivers, coast) build and stay. Bands on poor or variable land stay mobile. | built (2026-10-14); mobile test not yet passed: houses spread on dry land as on wet, because people are not food-limited |
| 2b | **Making staying a real question** | Only a share of plants is edible, by kind of country. Cold raises energy needs, against each kind's comfort and insulation. Houses come in two levels: brush shelters, and stone-footed houses that need stone and timber. Customs: leaving camp after a death against burying at home; demand sharing weakening stores; quarrels splitting big settled bands unless a custom keeps the peace. | The mobile test passes: houses are rarer on dry, poor land. People stay inside the record. | done (2026-10-14): stone-footed houses in 4–9% of bands, fewest on dry land; brush shelters everywhere; children reaching 15 in range, adult life still short (19–25 years left at 15) |
| 2c | **Conflict and its geography** | Hostility grows with social distance: strangers, other tongues, other customs, no ties, crowded country. Settled bands can be found: their house places are told along tie networks. Raids become planned expeditions to a remembered target, timed by moon and season, not only chance meetings. One fighting model, shared with hunting: numbers × weapons × skill, surprise from sight, cover, scouts, tracking and the moon, terrain from high ground, refuge and cover, and built defences (palisades, walls, ditches) paid in labour and materials. Mobile bands can flee; settled ones stand to lose stores and houses. Revenge feuds, alliances and avoiding are all choices for the learned ways. **Contested places:** rich, fixed spots (fisheries and spawning runs, oak and nut groves, springs in dry country, flint, fords and passes on herd routes, kite sites, high lookouts) are measured and remembered; bands claim the ground around their graves, houses and best spots; trespass raises hostility, and access is fought over, shared by ties, or yielded. **Caches are robbed**: by bands that find them, and by bears, hyenas, boar and rodents. | Resource value varies strongly across the map (measured: how unequal food per hex is, and where the richest spots are). Contests cluster at the richest spots. Violent deaths stay inside 2–15% for foragers and rise with settling and density. Defences appear where raids come. The learned ways find ambushes, high ground and alliances. | built (2026-10-14): violent deaths 8–12%, palisades where homes are raided, homes known along ties; the richest 5% of hexes hold 23% of the food (Gini 0.45); raiding and fortifying are learned ways, still to be trained |
| 3 | **Crops by their properties** | Plant foods get traits: annual grass that shatters, tuber grown from cuttings, tree nut; storage life; processing needs. Harvesting with sickles selects for ears that don't shatter. Sowing, weeding and burning are choices with delayed yields. | Domestication drifts only where stands are harvested with sickles and resown. Cultivation is taken up only where it pays. | first part built (2026-10-14): wild cereal stands by climate, shattering, sickle selection, autumn sowing at home; selection slowed to the archaeological pace (one to two thousand years for non-shattering ears to dominate). Still to come: tubers and nuts by their properties, weeding and burning |
| 4 | **Processing: containers, bread, beer** | Grinding and baking cut handling time and raise value. Brewing turns surplus grain into feast drink. Containers (baskets now, pottery later) enable boiling and storage. Storage techniques: drying, smoking, leaching. | Grain's share of the diet rises with processing tools. Feasts draw on brewed surplus. | first part built (2026-10-14): ground and baked grain feeds fully (raw 60%); beer brewed from stored grain at feasts binds guests half as much again |
| 5 | **Crafts, ornaments, exchange** | Bone tools, needles, beads and figurines made from hide, bone and sinew. Ornaments travel along ties as goods and show in burials as standing. | Exotic goods spread along tie networks. Inequality appears in grave goods. | built (2026-10-14): shell beads reach inland along ties (0.2 → 0.5 a person over 20 years, against 1 by the sea); the richest tenth of graves hold over half the grave goods |
| 6 | **Herd animals** | Managing wild goats, sheep, boar and aurochs: sparing females, culling young males, keeping animals near camp. Driven by each species' domestication traits. A herding-first path is possible. | Managed herds grow where the species allows. Dogs come first. | built (2026-10-14): bands at home keep young caught in the hunt; after 20 years about 230 kept boar in 29 bands, slowly growing tamer; dogs stay behind their switch |
| 7 | **Climate shock (Younger Dryas)** | A scripted cold, dry spell of about 1,200 years in the weather. | Bands become more mobile, or shift to stored and grown food. Nothing is forced. | |

**Baseline (next, 2026-10-15):** after an overnight training run on the server, save the most natural and best-performing Natufian models (the three role networks and the band ways) as a frozen, named baseline. Record the commit, settings, seeds and the check's record line with them, so later changes are compared against it.

**Test cases running through all steps:**
- **Jomon test:** on a coast- or oak-rich map, bands may settle without crops. That must be possible.
- **Mobile test:** on poor, variable land, bands must stay mobile.
- **Record:** density, mobility, diet shares, health and violence against the ethnographic and archaeological ranges, through the check's record line.

**Supporting work, done when a step needs it:**
- the remembered-place memory (done: step 1 of [memory-and-telling.md](memory-and-telling.md));
- telling places by description;
- the learned ways growing new outputs for each new choice.
