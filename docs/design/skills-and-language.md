# Stone Age skills, and a language to share them

Proposal, 2026-10-12. Nothing here is built.

## Two halves of a skill

Every skill below has two parts, and the model keeps them apart.

- **Knowing** (*K*): that a thing can be done, and the facts it rests on. "Flint breaks sharp." "Acorns are food once soaked." "Aurochs drink at the bend at dusk." This part can be put into words. It spreads by talk, between groups as well as within one, and can be wrong.
- **Doing** (*D*): how it is done, held in the hands and eyes. Knapping a blade from a core, reading a day-old track, keeping an ember alive on the march. Words help only a little. It spreads by watching and practising beside someone who has it, it takes years, and it fades without use.

The literature on Stone Age learning stresses this split. Experiments in learning to knap show that being told what to do helps much less than practice with a skilled model. So in the model, telling passes on *K* but never *D*. A group that hears of the bow and arrow knows it exists, but it still has to learn to make and use one.

Each skill also carries:
- **Needs:** materials, tools and other skills that must come first.
- **Learning:** how it is passed on. **told** means *K* is shareable in words. **shown** means *D* needs a teacher present. **found** means it can be discovered alone, given the right exposure.
- **Era:** when the record first shows it widely. LP is the Lower Palaeolithic (before about 300 ka), MP the Middle Palaeolithic (300–50 ka), UP the Upper Palaeolithic (50–20 ka), EP the Epipalaeolithic and Natufian (20–11.7 ka) and N the Neolithic. Natufian-era bands, the simulation's start, have everything up to EP.

Dates and attributions here are from memory, not looked up. They should be checked before being treated as calibrated.

### Each quarry and each food is its own skill
The hunting methods above are general. On top of them, each band holds a skill per kind of quarry, and the same for foraging.
- **Hunting each species.** Hunting ibex, gazelle, aurochs, boar, hare, waterfowl, fish or seals are separate skills, one per species in the simulation.
  - *Knowing:* where and when it feeds, drinks, rests and moves; what it fears; which wind gives you away; how it defends itself (aurochs turn and charge, boar ambush, ibex climb out of reach); which method works on it; and how it behaves in each season and at each moon.
  - *Doing:* approaching it on its own ground, the shot or thrust that drops it, and handling it when it fights back.
  - Success against a species depends on the band's craft for that species, and every hunt of it, kill or miss, is practice.
- **Foraging each food.** Acorns and nuts, wild cereals, bulbs and roots, fruit and berries, greens, mushrooms, honey, eggs, shellfish and insects are separate skills.
  - *Knowing:* where each grows, when it is ripe, the look-alikes and poisons, and how much a patch yields before it needs rest.
  - *Doing:* finding it quickly, harvesting without waste, and processing it (leaching acorns, threshing grain, digging bulbs).
- **Craft gathers where a band lives.** A band that has wintered on acorns and gazelle for generations is expert at both and clumsy with fish. A band moving to new country has to relearn its quarry and plants, by practice and by talk with the bands already there.

## How the model would carry skills

- **Per band, per skill:**
  - *knows* is whether the band holds *K*.
  - *belief* is how sure the band is, for things heard.
  - *craft* is *D*, from 0 to 1: how well the band can actually do it.
- **Effects:** the outcome of a skilled act depends on *craft*. A band with the bow but low *craft* misses most shots and breaks arrows. *Craft* also caps the quality of what is made: a poor knapper's blades are short and break.
- **Learning *D*:** *craft* rises with practice, every time the skill is used, whether or not anyone teaches. Trial and error works alone, just more slowly and with more failures; a skilled teacher in the same band speeds it up several times. *Craft* falls slowly without use.
- **Practice also teaches *K*:** doing a thing often turns up new facts and refinements. A knapper finds that heated flint works better; a hunter learns where gazelle cross. Practice is a source of *K*, not only of *craft*, and that *K* can then be told to others.
- **Losing skills:** small or isolated bands can lose skills when the last skilled member dies. This is the "Tasmanian effect" of the literature: population size and contact sustain complex skills.
- **Discovery:** *K* can be found by chance, with odds raised by exposure: seeing grass grow back after a lightning fire, or seeds sprouting by a midden.
- **Prerequisites:** *needs* sets the order skills can be learned in. Tools and materials gate skills.
- **Objects:** tools are things, made with skill from materials found in the world. The species products (meat, fat, hide, bone, sinew, antler, fur), minerals (flint, obsidian, ochre) and plants already exist for this. Tools wear out and are carried, cached and traded.

## A small language

Humans talk, and what they say carries information they cannot sense directly, which the animals' keen senses never needed.

**Words name what the world has:**
- *Things:* every species, plant form and food, rock and mineral, water feature (river, spring, lake, sea), landform (hill, cliff, valley, marsh) and weather (wind from a quarter, rain, snow, storm, heat).
- *Time:* day, night, moon and its phases, season, counts of days and moons, "before" and "after".
- *Places:* names a band gives its own landmarks (this spring, that cliff), plus directions (sunrise, sunset, the north wind's quarter, uphill, downstream).
- *Actions:* go, hunt, gather, make, eat, drink, fight, give, burn, cross, stay, flee.
- *Qualities:* many and few, near and far, good and bad, big and small, dangerous and safe.
- *People:* this band, that band, names.

**Sentences** are short frames of those words:
- **Sighting:** what, how many, where, when. "Aurochs many at Reed-Bend, last moon."
- **Rule:** if this, then that. "South wind, then rain." "Full moon, then lions hunt less."
- **How-to (*K* only):** what can be done with what. "Flint, heat in sand, then knaps well."
- **Proposal:** "We go to Reed-Bend at new moon." "Give obsidian for hides."

**Each band has its own words.** When bands meet, they talk, and two things happen:
- **Information passes,** weighed by trust and by how old the news is. Sightings, routes, dangers, rules and *K* of skills all travel this way. Hearsay can be wrong or out of date.
- **Words converge.** This is a naming game, after Steels' experiments: a word that works in an exchange is reinforced, and one that fails is dropped or replaced.
  - Bands that meet often come to share words; bands that never meet drift apart.
  - Dialect regions emerge along whatever corridors people actually travel. That is the cultural-basin effect you raised for the wide east–west map, and it can be measured.
  - Misunderstanding is real: a sighting told in a half-shared language may name the wrong place.

**Inside a band,** language carries *K* and helps teaching. Stories keep *K* alive across generations even when no one has practised it for a while, though *craft* still has to be relearned.

## Prediction and time for humans

Humans get no foresight. The animals' `foresight` term is not available to them. Instead they observe:
- **Sky:** the moon phase, which now drifts against the seasons; day length and where the sun rises; the wind's quarter and strength; and clouds, a noisy sign of the coming regime.
- **Animals:** migrating birds arriving or leaving, herds moving ahead of snow, and the like.

From these they form rules. A rule is a pattern seen to hold often enough: "south wind and high cloud → rain within days". Rules can be learned, held as *K*, shared in speech, and they can be wrong. Calendars are the same. Counting moons predicts the seasons only roughly, because the moon drifts about 6 days a year against the 360-day year. Bands that also watch the sun or the stars, or add a moon now and then, keep better time. Better time means arriving at the fruiting trees or the gazelle migration on time.

## Build order

1. **Bands as groups** on the weekly clock: camp choice, foraging and hunting with a few hard-coded skills, products, and fire for cooking and burning.
2. **Skills as data:** *K* and *craft* per band, prerequisites, tools as objects that wear out, and learning by practice.
3. **Observation and rules:** what bands can see each week, and learned rules for weather and calendars.
4. **Language:** a vocabulary per band, meetings, information exchange, and the naming game.
5. **Trade and alliances**, then settling, storage, cultivation and herding.
