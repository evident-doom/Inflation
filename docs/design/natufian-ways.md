# Natufian ways, and how bands might come to them

Notes and proposal, 2026-10-14. Nothing here is built.

Each point is marked by how sure we can be:
- **(fact)** is attested in the archaeology;
- **(likely)** is the common reading of it;
- **(guess)** is analogy with recent foragers.

## What we know

### When and where
- **(fact)** The Natufian ran from about 15,000 to 11,500 years ago in the Levant.
- **(fact)** The Early Natufian had hamlets of round, half-sunken stone houses, for example at Ain Mallaha and Hayonim. The Late Natufian, in the colder, drier Younger Dryas, was more mobile.
- **(fact)** The Pre-Pottery Neolithic followed, with Göbekli Tepe's great enclosures in the north.

### Food
- **(fact)** Gazelle dominates the bones, with fallow deer, boar, aurochs, hares, birds, tortoises and fish.
- **(fact)** Sickle blades carry the gloss of cutting wild cereals.
- **(fact)** Mortars, pestles and bedrock cup-holes were used to pound acorns, almonds and seed.
- **(fact)** House mice and sparrows appear: the first commensals, a sign of people staying put.
- **(likely)** Stores of nuts and grain carried bands through lean seasons.
- **(guess)** Women did most of the plant work, as among recent foragers.

### The dead
- **(fact)** The dead were buried under house floors and in cave mouths, often many together.
- **(fact)** Some burials are richly adorned. At el-Wad there are headdresses and belts of dentalium shell from the Mediterranean and Red Sea.
- **(fact)** At Ain Mallaha an old person was buried with a hand on a puppy.
- **(fact)** At Hilazon Tachtit a disabled woman was buried with more than 70 tortoise shells, an eagle wing, a boar's arm bone, a leopard's pelvis and a human foot. Beside her was a pit with the remains of a large feast of tortoises and wild cattle. She is read as a shaman, and it is the earliest clear feast.
- **(fact)** At Raqefet graves were lined with flowers (sage, mint). Stone mortars there may have brewed beer from wild grain (likely, debated).
- **(fact)** In the Late Natufian skulls were removed and reburied.
- **(likely)** That was an ancestor cult, and it carried into the Neolithic plastered skulls.

### Art and exchange
- **(fact)** Carved bone sickle hafts show animals, mostly young ruminants.
- **(fact)** There are small stone figurines, including the Ain Sakhri lovers.
- **(fact)** Dentalium shells travelled more than 100 km, Anatolian obsidian very rarely, and basalt from its sources.
- **(likely)** Exchange ran through networks of kin and partners, with goods passed hand to hand.

### Meeting places
- **(fact)** Some sites are far larger and richer than the rest, with many burials, feasting remains and imported shell.
- **(likely)** These were aggregation sites, where several bands came together in season.
- **(guess)** Bands met at those sites when food was plentiful: acorn fall in autumn, cereals in spring, gazelle passing through. The gatherings were for marriage, exchange, settling disputes, burying the dead and feasting with the ancestors.

### Beliefs
- **(likely)** Animals mattered as spirits: the shaman's grave goods, the decorated hafts, the puppy burial.
- **(likely)** The ancestors held claim to ground, and burial places marked whose country it was.
- **(guess)** Food taboos and sharing rules, as among recent foragers. Examples are young or pregnant animals spared, meat shared by set rules, and some animals avoided by some people.

## How such ways form, and what they do

Recent foragers show the mechanisms: customs that pay off survive and spread, and costly ones last when they buy something.
- **Feasts** turn a surplus that would spoil into ties: alliances, marriages, help in bad years.
- **Burials at a place** stake a claim to it and draw the band back.
- **Shared calendars**, such as moons, the solstice and the cranes' arrival, let scattered bands meet without messengers.
- **Exchange of rare things** (shell, obsidian) keeps partnerships alive between meetings.
- **Taboos** often protect a resource, like sparing young or breeding animals, or keep the peace, for example through rules on sharing.

## What we have now

Customs exist in the model as on/off traits: burial, sky lore, sparing the young, oral tradition, feasts, medicine, exogamy, hygiene and sharing.
- Bands take them up and drop them at set rates.
- Each has a fixed effect.

Meetings happen when bands' weekly ranges overlap, with a pull towards springs and lakes. No band aims for a meeting.

## Proposal: customs as choices with real costs and gains

1. **Ties between bands.** Each band remembers each band it has met:
   - how well they get on, raised by gifts, feasts, marriages and help, and lowered by raids and theft;
   - which tongue they speak.

   Ties make raids rarer, sharing in hunger likelier and telling of country fuller. They fade unless renewed.
2. **Gatherings as places and times worth going to.** The gains rise with the number of bands present:
   - **marriage partners:** inbreeding is already modelled, so a larger pool is a real gain;
   - **exchange:** of materials and rare goods;
   - **news:** told places, routes and the seasons;
   - **ties:** renewed at the gathering;
   - **feasting:** turning surplus stores into ties.

   The costs are real too: crowding, outbreaks (the plague mechanism already exists), and food run down around the site. That food cost is why gatherings follow plenty.
3. **Burials make places.** Burying the dead at a site makes it an ancestral place in the band's memory, a token per the memory design. Its pull grows with the dead buried there, and other bands may read it as a claim.
4. **Calendars.** A recurring time, such as the fifth new moon or the crane's return, plus an ancestral or plentiful place, makes an appointment. Bands holding the same appointment go there, and sky lore makes the timing sharper.
5. **Taboos and rules as policy settings with consequences:**
   - sparing young animals (already in hunting) lets herds recover;
   - sharing rules spread the risk;
   - avoidance rules leave an animal or place alone.

   Bands try them, and whether they pay shows up in survival. Copying from bands they have ties with replaces the fixed take-up rates.

## Encouraging gatherings without scripting them

- **Make them pay:** steps 1 and 2. If marriage, exchange and news are real gains, the learned policy will find the gatherings.
- **The scaffold's default:** a band with feasting and a store above its aim heads for the season's richest known place nearby. The hand-written behaviour then produces gatherings, and the networks copy it as their starting point.
- **Schelling points:** conspicuous places (lake shores, springs, crags, burial sites) and conspicuous times (solstice, new moon, the cranes) make it easy for bands to choose the same place and time without arranging it.
