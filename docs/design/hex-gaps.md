# Hex model: what is still missing, and the road to a balanced, closed ecology

13 Oct 2026. The 1 km movement model is gone; its features are listed against the hex model in
`hex-port-status.md`. This is the work list that follows from it, in the order it will be done,
before people come onto the hexes.

## 1. Missing features, in order

Ground up, the ones that tie the layers together first.

1. **Nutrients round the loop.** Dung and urine give back most of what grazers eat; carcasses and
   dead plants rot into the soil; soil organic matter sets how much the plants can grow. Today the
   soil life builds organic matter from litter and carcasses, but what animals eat leaves the
   system.
2. **No life from nothing.** Today several fields have floors (insects, rodents, reptiles,
   pollinators, foxes, eagles, birds) that put animals back where they died out. Empty places
   should be refilled only from neighbouring places, or not at all.
3. **Fire and the animals.** Fire kills and drives off herds and packs, as it already does the
   small life; the burn greens up with rich grass that draws grazers.
4. **Flood, snow and ash deaths; snow making travel harder.**
5. **The year's rhythm.** Breeding seasons shifting towards the months that feed mothers best;
   autumn fattening; bears sleeping through the winter on their fat; winter coats grown and moulted.
6. **Food as separate pools.** Roots, mast, fruit and standing straw as stocks that build up and
   are eaten down, instead of read off the standing plants; roughage digested by body size;
   cliffs keeping all but climbers off part of the food.
7. **Animals shaping the plants.** Boar rooting, fruit-eaters carrying seed, trampling and worn
   trails (the plants already respond; nothing sets them yet).
8. **Hunters among themselves.** Bigger hunters stealing kills and now and then killing smaller
   ones; scent marks instead of exclusive ground; dangerous prey fighting back; speed in the chase.
9. **Sensing what is coming.** Rain ahead in a dry spell, floods and cold snaps a week ahead.
10. **Water life.** Fish in rivers, lakes and the sea, shellfish, the spring spawning runs up the
    rivers, crocodiles; then upwelling, plankton, sea mammals, sharks and sea-bird colonies.
11. **Volcanoes.** Eruptions, ash falling downwind, volcanic winters.

Left for when people come: dams, canals and irrigation; animals following camps; boldness and
fear of people; learned bands.

## 2. A closed system

Only these act on the world from outside, each from the climate and its weather:

- Warmth (sunlight), as temperature, and day length.
- Rain and snow.
- Wind.
- Weather shocks: droughts, cold winters, heat waves (and later volcanic winters).

Everything else flows between layers inside: plants take water from the soil and nutrients from
organic matter; grazers eat plants and give dung back; hunters eat grazers; everything dies into
carrion and litter; the soil life turns those back into organic matter. Each layer's numbers come
from what it ate, and a perturbation (a drought, a fire, a crash) runs through the others.

The audit for each step: no number appears from nowhere, no floor, and each layer's losses go
somewhere.

## 3. Balance

When the features are in: an extended run (100 years or more, full map, no people), to see where
each layer settles and whether it stays there. The settled numbers become the starting state of
new worlds, so a run starts near its balance instead of spending decades getting there. Species
that find no niche are let go (see the niche policy); unbounded growth gets a real regulator.
